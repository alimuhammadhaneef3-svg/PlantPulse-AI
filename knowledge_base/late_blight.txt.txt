DISEASE: Tomato Late Blight

CATEGORY: Tomato Plant Disease

1. OVERVIEW

Late Blight is a destructive tomato disease caused by Phytophthora infestans. It is an oomycete (water mold), not a true fungus.

The disease can spread rapidly under cool, humid and wet environmental conditions. It can affect leaves, stems and tomato fruits.

2. SYMPTOMS

- Irregular, water-soaked green or dark patches appear on leaves.
- Infected areas gradually turn brown or black.
- White growth may appear on the underside of infected leaves under humid conditions.
- Stems may develop dark brown or black lesions.
- Fruits can develop firm, brown, irregular lesions.
- Severe infections can destroy large portions of the plant.

3. FAVORABLE CONDITIONS

- Cool temperatures.
- High humidity.
- Frequent rainfall.
- Heavy dew.
- Prolonged leaf wetness.
- Poor air circulation.

4. DISEASE SPREAD

Phytophthora infestans spreads through spores carried by wind, rain and water.

Infected tomato plants, potato plants, volunteer plants and infected planting materials can contribute to disease spread.

5. MANAGEMENT AND TREATMENT

- Regularly inspect tomato plants for suspicious symptoms.
- Remove infected plant material carefully.
- Avoid moving infected plant debris between fields.
- Maintain sufficient spacing between plants.
- Improve air circulation.
- Avoid overhead irrigation where possible.
- Remove volunteer potato and tomato plants that may act as disease sources.
- Use disease-free transplants.
- Consider locally suitable tolerant varieties where available.
- Consult an agricultural specialist about appropriate fungicide options.
- Use only products registered for the crop and disease in the local area.
- Follow the pesticide label, protective equipment requirements and pre-harvest interval.

6. PREVENTION

- Use healthy transplants.
- Monitor plants frequently during wet weather.
- Maintain good field sanitation.
- Avoid excessive leaf wetness.
- Practice suitable crop rotation.
- Remove infected crop debris.
- Maintain adequate spacing and airflow.
- Monitor nearby potato and tomato crops for disease outbreaks.

7. IMPORTANT DIFFERENCE: EARLY BLIGHT VS LATE BLIGHT

Early Blight:
- Usually produces brown spots with concentric rings.
- Commonly starts on older lower leaves.
- Caused by Alternaria species.

Late Blight:
- Produces irregular, water-soaked dark lesions.
- Can spread very rapidly in favorable weather.
- Caused by Phytophthora infestans.

8. IMPORTANT NOTE

Late Blight can resemble other tomato diseases. A visual prediction alone cannot confirm the disease with certainty.

If symptoms are unclear or the disease is spreading rapidly, consult an agricultural specialist or plant pathologist.
